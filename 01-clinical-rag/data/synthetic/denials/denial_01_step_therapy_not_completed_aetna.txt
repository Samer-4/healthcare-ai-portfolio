**REASON FOR DENIAL: Step Therapy Requirements Not Completed**

Coverage for **Wegovy® (semaglutide 2.4 mg)** is subject to Aetna's **Clinical Policy Bulletin No. 0580 – Obesity Management Pharmacotherapy**, effective January 1, 2025. Pursuant to this policy, authorization for branded GLP-1 receptor agonist therapies indicated for chronic weight management requires documented completion of required step therapy prior to approval of the requested agent.

Based on the clinical documentation submitted, the following required step therapy criteria have **not been satisfied:**

**1. Prior Trial and Failure of a Lower-Tier Pharmacotherapy Agent**
The member's clinical record does not contain adequate documentation demonstrating a minimum **twelve (12)-week trial** with a formulary preferred weight management agent, such as:

- **Orlistat** (Xenical®/Alli®) – Tier 1 preferred, or
- **Phentermine/topiramate ER** (Qsymia®) – Tier 2 preferred, or
- **Bupropion/naltrexone** (Contrave®) – Tier 2 preferred

Documentation must demonstrate that such agent(s) were prescribed at a therapeutic dose, that the member demonstrated adequate adherence, and that the trial resulted in less than five percent (5%) total body weight loss, was discontinued due to documented adverse effects, or is otherwise clinically contraindicated.

**2. Structured Weight Management Program Participation**
Per Clinical Policy Bulletin No. 0580, Section IV.B.2, the member must have documented participation of no less than **six (6) months** in a structured, provider-supervised behavioral weight management program (e.g., a program incorporating dietary counseling, physical activity guidance, and behavioral modification), within the twenty-four (24) months preceding the request date. The submitted documentation does not include evidence of enrollment in or completion of such a program.

**3. Regarding Type 2 Diabetes Diagnosis**
Aetna acknowledges that the member carries a diagnosis of Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (E11.9) with a reported BMI of 38 kg/m². Aetna notes that semaglutide in lower doses is available as **Ozempic
