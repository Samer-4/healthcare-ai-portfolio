## HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS

Mr. Marcus Thompson is a 38-year-old male with a confirmed diagnosis of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease with hepatic steatosis (ICD-10: K76.0) and overweight status (ICD-10: E66.09, BMI 29 kg/m²) who presents for evaluation and treatment planning. His hepatic disease was identified approximately 18 months ago (December 2023) following elevated transaminases noted on routine laboratory screening: ALT 78 U/L, AST 62 U/L. Subsequent hepatic ultrasound demonstrated diffuse increased echogenicity consistent with moderate-to-severe hepatic steatosis. A FibroScan (transient elastography) performed in February 2024 revealed a controlled attenuation parameter (CAP) score of 298 dB/m, consistent with significant hepatic fat accumulation (S2–S3 steatosis), and a liver stiffness measurement (LSM) of 7.8 kPa, suggesting early fibrosis (F1–F2 range). Liver biopsy performed in May 2024 confirmed non-alcoholic steatohepatitis with a NAFLD activity score (NAS) of 5 (steatosis 2, lobular inflammation 2, hepatocyte ballooning 1) and stage F1 fibrosis. Alcohol use was thoroughly evaluated and excluded as a contributing etiology; Mr. Thompson reports alcohol consumption of fewer than two standard drinks per week, well below hepatotoxic thresholds. Viral hepatitis serologies (hepatitis B surface antigen, hepatitis C antibody), autoimmune hepatitis panel, alpha-1 antitrypsin levels, ceruloplasmin, ferritin/transferrin saturation, and thyroid function tests were all within normal limits or negative, confirming NASH as the primary etiology.

Over the past 18 months, Mr. Thompson has been actively engaged in structured lifestyle modification under the supervision of his primary care physician and a registered dietitian. Beginning in January 2024, he was enrolled in a formal 12-week intensive behavioral weight loss program incorporating a calorie-restricted diet (500–750 kcal/day deficit), increased physical activity (150+ minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise per week), and behavioral counseling sessions every two weeks. Despite full adherence to the program, Mr. Thompson achieved only a modest 3% total body weight reduction (approximately 2.5 kg), falling short of the ≥7–10% weight loss threshold consistently demonstrated in clinical trials (including the NASH Clinical Research Network studies) to produce meaningful histologic improvement in NASH. Repeat ALT at the conclusion of the lifestyle program in April 2024 remained elevated at 68 U/L. He continued independent dietary and exercise efforts through the remainder of 2024 and into early 2025 with continued suboptimal weight loss response, attributed in part to underlying metabolic dysregulation. A repeat FibroScan in March 2025 showed persistence of significant hepatic steatosis (CAP 289 dB/m) and stable but not improved liver stiffness at 8.1 kPa. His most recent transaminases (May 2025) showed ALT 72 U/L and AST 58 U/L, indicating persistent hepatocellular injury despite ongoing lifestyle efforts.

Given the failure of structured lifestyle intervention to achieve sufficient weight loss or biochemical/radiologic improvement in his NASH, and recognizing the progressive natural history of untreated NASH with the risk of advancing fibrosis, cirrhosis, hepatocellular carcinoma, and liver-related mortality, pharmacologic therapy is now clearly indicated. The GLP-1 receptor agonist class, and specifically semaglutide, has accumulated the most robust and compelling clinical evidence base for the treatment of NASH among all available pharmacologic agents to date. The landmark NASH resolution trial of subcutaneous semaglutide 0.4 mg daily (Newsome et al., *New England Journal of Medicine*, 2021) demonstrated NASH resolution without worsening of fibrosis in 59% of patients treated with semaglutide versus 17% in the placebo arm (p < 0.001). Additionally, the Phase III ESSENCE trial (presented 2024) evaluating semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly demonstrated statistically significant improvements in NASH resolution and, notably, fibrosis regression in biopsy-confirmed NASH patients. These data provide strong, high-quality clinical evidence supporting semaglutide's efficacy specifically in Mr. Thompson's confirmed diagnosis of NASH/NAFLD with histologic activity.
