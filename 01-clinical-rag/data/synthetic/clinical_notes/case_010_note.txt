## HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS

Ms. Davis is a 44-year-old female with a longstanding history of type 2 diabetes mellitus first diagnosed in March 2018 and morbid obesity (current BMI 40 kg/m²), who presents today for endocrinology consultation regarding her suboptimal glycemic control and continued weight-related morbidity. She has been under the care of Dr. Nguyen for the past six years and has carried an escalating burden of metabolic disease during that period. Her most recent HbA1c, drawn June 25, 2025, was 9.1%, reflecting persistently uncontrolled hyperglycemia despite multiple medication adjustments. She also carries a diagnosis of essential hypertension, currently managed with amlodipine and lisinopril, though her blood pressures have remained above target on recent readings. The patient describes fatigue, polyuria, polydipsia, and difficulty with sustained activity due to her weight.

The patient and her primary care provider have engaged in structured, documented efforts to manage her obesity and diabetes with conservative measures prior to requesting pharmacologic intensification with a GLP-1/GIP receptor agonist. Ms. Davis was enrolled in a formal supervised medical weight management program through her health system from September 2022 through March 2023 (six months), during which she received structured dietary counseling, individualized caloric restriction guidance (1,400 kcal/day low-glycemic index diet), and supervised aerobic and resistance exercise programming three times per week. She achieved a maximum weight loss of 9 lbs (approximately 3% of body weight) during this period, well below the threshold of clinical meaningfulness for a patient with her degree of obesity and metabolic comorbidity. She was formally assessed by a registered dietitian on a monthly basis throughout this program, with all sessions documented in the shared medical record. Following discharge from the program, she attempted to maintain the dietary and exercise modifications independently but regained the lost weight within four months, with her weight returning to baseline by August 2023.

With regard to pharmacologic management, Ms. Davis was initiated on semaglutide injection (Ozempic, 0.25 mg subcutaneously weekly, titrated to 0.5 mg weekly at week 4, and subsequently to 1.0 mg weekly at week 12) in November 2023 by Dr. Nguyen. Semaglutide is understood to be the covered alternative cited in the UnitedHealthcare denial. After six months of therapy at the 1.0 mg dose (through May 2024), her HbA1c improved only modestly from 9.4% to 8.9% — a reduction of 0.5%, which remains markedly above the ADA-recommended target of less than 7.0% for her age group. Furthermore, her weight loss on semaglutide 1.0 mg over this same six-month period was 6 lbs (2.4% of body weight), which is clinically insufficient given her BMI of 40 kg/m² and the degree of weight reduction required to meaningfully reduce her cardiometabolic risk. The dose was further escalated to semaglutide 2.0 mg subcutaneously weekly in June 2024 and continued for an additional five months through October 2024. At that dose, she experienced persistent nausea rated 6/10 in severity, intermittent vomiting (two to three episodes per week), and early satiety to a degree that significantly impaired her nutritional intake and quality of life, as documented in clinical notes dated July 9, 2024, August 22, 2024, and October 3, 2024. Despite these side effects limiting compliance with the 2.0 mg dose, HbA1c at the conclusion of this trial (October 2024) was 8.7%, and her weight was essentially unchanged relative to the 1.0 mg endpoint. Semaglutide was discontinued in October 2024 due to inadequate efficacy and dose-limiting gastrointestinal intolerance.
