## HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS

Mr. Okafor is a 52-year-old male with a longstanding history of primary hypertension (ICD-10: I10) and class I obesity (ICD-10: E66.09) who presents for formal obesity medicine evaluation. His weight history is notable for a gradual increase over the past 12–15 years, with his current weight of 231 lbs and height of 5'9" yielding a BMI of 35 kg/m². He reports that his highest lifetime weight was approximately 245 lbs approximately two years ago, achieved prior to initiating structured lifestyle modifications. Despite significant personal investment in dietary changes and physical activity, he has been unable to achieve or sustain clinically meaningful weight reduction below his current plateau. His obesity directly contributes to his hypertension, which has been increasingly difficult to control over the same time period and currently requires two antihypertensive agents. He reports persistent fatigue, exertional dyspnea with moderate activity, and bilateral knee discomfort consistent with obesity-related mechanical stress, all of which limit his capacity to engage in vigorous exercise programs.

The patient has been enrolled in a structured behavioral weight loss program through his primary care clinic since January 2024, consisting of individualized dietitian-supervised dietary counseling with a caloric restriction target of 500–750 kcal/day deficit, behavioral modification sessions, and a supervised walking program initiated at 150 minutes per week. He has maintained adherence to this program for approximately 17 months. Despite these efforts, total weight loss has been approximately 14 lbs (6% of body weight) from a peak of 245 lbs, and weight has been stable at 231 lbs for the past five to six months, indicating a physiological plateau consistent with known adaptive thermogenesis. His referring physician, Dr. Hensley, has documented multiple office visits between February 2024 and May 2025 reflecting ongoing counseling, encouragement, and pharmacologic consideration. Orlistat 120 mg three times daily was trialed from August 2024 through January 2025 (six months); the patient experienced inadequate weight loss (net loss of 3 lbs over the full course of therapy) along with significant gastrointestinal side effects including steatorrhea, fecal urgency, and oily spotting, ultimately impairing occupational functioning and necessitating discontinuation. Phentermine/topiramate extended-release was considered but was deferred given the patient's established hypertension and the known risk of cardiovascular adverse effects with sympathomimetic agents in this population.

Given the patient's age, sex, BMI of 35 kg/m² with obesity-related comorbidities (hypertension), documented failure of sustained behavioral intervention, and failure/intolerance of an alternative FDA-approved weight loss pharmacotherapy, he meets clinical and regulatory criteria for advanced pharmacologic intervention. Tirzepatide (Zepbound) is being requested as the next appropriate therapeutic step. As a dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist with an FDA-approved indication for chronic weight management in adults with a BMI ≥30 kg/m² or ≥27 kg/m² with at least one weight-related comorbidity, tirzepatide represents an evidence-based, guideline-concordant intervention for this patient. The SURMOUNT-1 trial demonstrated mean weight reductions of up to 20.9% with tirzepatide 15 mg weekly, substantially superior to all currently available alternatives. Improved weight control is additionally expected to produce clinically meaningful reductions in systolic blood pressure, potentially enabling de-escalation of antihypertensive therapy and reducing long-term cardiovascular risk.
