## HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS

Ms. Sandra K. Williams is a 49-year-old woman with a longstanding history of hypothyroidism and obesity who presents for endocrinology consultation regarding medical management of her weight. She has struggled with progressive weight gain over the past 12 years, with a documented peak weight of 218 lbs (BMI 36 kg/m²) recorded in January 2025. Her thyroid function has been monitored closely and her hypothyroidism is currently well controlled on levothyroxine 100 mcg daily, with a most recent TSH of 1.8 mIU/L (March 2025), effectively excluding undertreated hypothyroidism as the sole driver of her weight. Despite thyroid optimization, she has continued to gain approximately 6–8 lbs per year over the past three years and has been unable to achieve or sustain meaningful weight reduction through lifestyle measures alone.

Ms. Williams has engaged in a structured, multidisciplinary weight management program through her primary care clinic. She was enrolled in a supervised low-calorie dietary program with formal dietitian counseling, which she initiated in February 2023 and continued through August 2023 (six months of documented participation). During this period she achieved a maximum weight loss of 9 lbs, which was entirely regained within four months of program completion. She subsequently participated in a supervised exercise program (three sessions per week, monitored by a certified exercise physiologist) from October 2023 through March 2024, with minimal sustained weight loss of 4 lbs at program end and full weight regain by July 2024. With respect to pharmacologic step therapy, Ms. Williams was started on orlistat 120 mg three times daily with meals in September 2023 (overlapping with continued dietary intervention). After 16 weeks of therapy through January 2024, she achieved less than 2% total body weight loss and reported significant GI intolerability — including fecal urgency, oily stool, and fecal incontinence — which substantially impaired her quality of life and occupational functioning. Orlistat was discontinued in January 2024 on these grounds.

Following the failure of orlistat, her PCP initiated phentermine/topiramate extended-release (Qsymia) 3.75 mg/23 mg daily in April 2024, titrated to the maintenance dose of 7.5 mg/46 mg by May 2024. Ms. Williams remained on this regimen through October 2024 (approximately six months at therapeutic dose). She achieved a modest 4.5 lb reduction in weight but subsequently developed paresthesias in bilateral upper extremities, word-finding difficulty, and mood lability, adverse effects consistent with the known topiramate component. These symptoms prompted discontinuation of phentermine/topiramate in October 2024. Bupropion/naltrexone (Contrave) was subsequently trialed beginning December 2024, titrated per protocol to 16 mg/180 mg twice daily by January 2025. After 14 weeks of therapy through March 2025, she experienced no clinically meaningful weight loss (net change: +0.8 lbs) and reported persistent nausea, headache, and dizziness that did not resolve with continued use. Contrave was discontinued in March 2025. She is now referred for consideration of a GLP-1/GIP receptor agonist, specifically tirzepatide (Zepbound), as the next appropriate step in evidence-based obesity pharmacotherapy.
