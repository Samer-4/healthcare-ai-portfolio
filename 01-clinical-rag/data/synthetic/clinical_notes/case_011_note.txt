## HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS

Mr. Marcus Thompson is a 38-year-old male with a documented history of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) and overweight status (BMI 29 kg/m²) who initially presented to his primary care physician in March 2023 with complaints of persistent right upper quadrant discomfort, fatigue, and elevated hepatic transaminases noted on routine laboratory evaluation. At that time, AST was 67 U/L and ALT was 89 U/L (both greater than twice the upper limit of normal). Hepatitis B surface antigen, hepatitis C antibody, anti-nuclear antibody, anti-smooth muscle antibody, and alpha-1 antitrypsin phenotyping were all negative, effectively excluding viral, autoimmune, and hereditary etiologies. Alcohol use was quantified at fewer than two standard drinks per week and deemed clinically non-contributory. Abdominal ultrasound performed April 2023 demonstrated diffuse hepatic echogenicity consistent with moderate-to-severe steatosis. Subsequent MRI with PDFF (proton density fat fraction) quantification obtained June 2023 confirmed hepatic fat fraction of 22%, meeting criteria for Grade 2–3 hepatic steatosis. A FibroScan (vibration-controlled transient elastography) performed July 2023 yielded a controlled attenuation parameter (CAP) score of 318 dB/m, further corroborating significant hepatic fat deposition, with a liver stiffness measurement of 7.2 kPa, indicating early fibrosis in the F1–F2 range. The patient was counseled extensively on lifestyle modification, dietary optimization, and weight reduction as the cornerstone of NAFLD management.

From August 2023 through June 2024 — a period of approximately 10 months — Mr. Thompson was enrolled in a structured, multidisciplinary lifestyle intervention program. He participated in weekly dietary counseling sessions with a registered dietitian focusing on a Mediterranean-pattern diet, caloric restriction targeting a 500–750 kcal/day deficit, and reduction of refined carbohydrates and saturated fats. He also undertook a supervised exercise program consisting of 150–200 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week in accordance with AASLD and EASL guidelines. Adherence was monitored via food and activity logs reviewed at monthly clinical visits. Despite sustained compliance, his weight decreased by only 3.1 kg (approximately 4.2% of initial body weight) — well below the 7–10% weight loss threshold consistently associated with histologic improvement in NASH/NAFLD. Follow-up laboratory studies in July 2024 demonstrated persistent transaminase elevation (AST 59 U/L, ALT 76 U/L), and repeat FibroScan in September 2024 showed a CAP score of 307 dB/m with liver stiffness now measured at 7.8 kPa, suggesting no meaningful regression of hepatic steatosis and possible progression of fibrosis. Vitamin E 800 IU daily was initiated in October 2023 for non-diabetic NAFLD per AASLD recommendations; after 12 months of therapy through October 2024, hepatic enzyme elevations and steatosis imaging findings remained unresolved. Pioglitazone was considered but declined by the patient due to concerns regarding weight gain and bladder cancer risk. Obeticholic acid is not approved for NAFLD/NASH outside of clinical trials and was not an appropriate option for this patient.

Given the failure of first-line lifestyle modification, the incomplete response to Vitamin E, and the progressive nature of his hepatic disease — with early fibrosis now documented — escalation of pharmacologic therapy is clinically imperative. GLP-1 receptor agonists, specifically semaglutide, have emerged as the most evidence-supported pharmacologic intervention for NAFLD/NASH. The NASH CRN (NASH Clinical Research Network) and the landmark Phase II NEJM-published trial by Newsome et al. (2021) demonstrated that semaglutide 2.4 mg subcutaneously weekly resulted in NASH resolution without worsening of fibrosis in 59% of treated participants versus 17% in the placebo arm (p<0.001). Furthermore, the ongoing Phase III ESSENCE trial evaluating semaglutide for NASH has demonstrated compelling interim efficacy data, and multiple GLP-1 receptor agonist studies (including liraglutide in the LEAN trial) have demonstrated histologic improvement in NASH independent of weight loss magnitude. The American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) 2023 Practice Guidance and the European Association for the Study of the Liver (EASL) 2024 Clinical Practice Guidelines both acknowledge GLP-1 receptor agonists as a rational pharmacologic strategy for NAFLD/NASH management in patients who fail lifestyle interventions. Accordingly, initiation of semaglutide is medically necessary, evidence-based, and the most appropriate next therapeutic step for Mr. Thompson.
