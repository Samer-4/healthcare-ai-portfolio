## HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS

Mr. Nguyen is a 60-year-old male with a current BMI of 30 kg/m² and established diagnoses of class I obesity (E66.09) and obstructive sleep apnea (G47.33). He presents with a longstanding history of weight excess dating back approximately 15 years, during which time he has made multiple structured and documented attempts at weight reduction. He enrolled in a medically supervised behavioral weight loss program through his primary care clinic from January 2022 through December 2022, incorporating a 1,200–1,400 kcal/day caloric-restriction diet plan designed by a registered dietitian and at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity physical activity per week. Despite full adherence for the duration of the program, his net weight loss was approximately 4 lbs, which he subsequently regained within six months of program completion. He was then prescribed orlistat 120 mg three times daily with meals beginning in March 2023, which he tolerated for approximately four months before discontinuing due to intolerable gastrointestinal adverse effects, including fecal urgency, oily spotting, and significant interference with daily activities and quality of life. He achieved no sustained or clinically meaningful weight reduction on orlistat.

His obstructive sleep apnea was formally diagnosed via in-laboratory polysomnography on September 7, 2023, with an apnea-hypopnea index (AHI) of 22 events/hour, consistent with moderate OSA. He was initiated on CPAP therapy at a pressure of 10 cmH₂O and has demonstrated reasonable adherence (≥4 hours/night on approximately 70% of nights per CPAP download data reviewed by pulmonology). However, his OSA remains inadequately controlled, with residual AHI averaging 9.4 events/hour and persistent daytime somnolence. His pulmonologist, Dr. Evelyn Tran, documented in her August 2024 note that weight reduction is the single most impactful modifiable intervention available to improve this patient's OSA severity and is strongly recommended as an adjunct to CPAP therapy. Multiple clinical trials, including the SCALE Sleep Apnea trial (Blackman et al., NEJM Evidence, 2023), have demonstrated that liraglutide 3.0 mg produces statistically significant and clinically meaningful reductions in AHI as well as body weight in patients with obesity and OSA, providing a dual therapeutic rationale for its use in this specific patient.

Liraglutide 3.0 mg/day (Saxenda) is FDA-approved specifically for chronic weight management in adults with a BMI ≥30 kg/m², or ≥27 kg/m² with at least one weight-related comorbidity, to be used in conjunction with a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity. Mr. Nguyen meets both criteria: his current BMI is 30 kg/m² and he carries a confirmed weight-related comorbidity in the form of obstructive sleep apnea. The use of liraglutide in this clinical context is therefore explicitly within the FDA-approved labeling of Saxenda and is not off-label. The prescribing information for Saxenda (Novo Nordisk, revised 2020) states: "SAXENDA is indicated as an adjunct to a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity for chronic weight management in adults with an initial body mass index (BMI) of... 30 kg/m² or greater (obese), or 27 kg/m² or greater (overweight) in the presence of at least one weight-related comorbid condition (e.g., hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, or dyslipidemia)." Obstructive sleep apnea is widely recognized as a weight-related comorbid condition. The denial characterization of this request as off-label is therefore clinically and factually inaccurate, and this note is submitted to support appeal and reversal of that determination.
