### History of Present Illness

Ms. Chen has been a patient at this practice since March 2021. She was initially referred by her OB/GYN for management of PCOS-associated metabolic dysfunction after lab work demonstrated fasting hyperglycemia and markedly elevated fasting insulin levels. Over the past four years, her obesity and insulin resistance have been the central drivers of worsening metabolic health, including progression to overt type 2 diabetes mellitus diagnosed formally on August 14, 2023, based on two separate fasting glucose values exceeding 126 mg/dL (132 mg/dL and 129 mg/dL) and an HbA1c of 7.4% confirmed on the same date.

Her PCOS was formally diagnosed in January 2019 by her gynecologist (Dr. Sonia Mehra, MD, OBGYN) based on Rotterdam criteria: oligomenorrhea, clinical hyperandrogenism (hirsutism, acne), and polycystic ovarian morphology on pelvic ultrasound. The hormonal and metabolic milieu of PCOS has significantly compounded her obesity and insulin resistance, creating a self-reinforcing cycle that has proven highly resistant to conventional therapies.

Ms. Chen reports years of diligent effort toward weight management and glycemic control. She describes persistent fatigue, difficulty with satiety, strong carbohydrate cravings she attributes to hormonal dysregulation, irregular menses (cycles ranging 45–90 days), and progressive weight gain despite what she characterizes as sustained lifestyle efforts. She reports no current tobacco use and alcohol use limited to social occasions, approximately 2–3 drinks per month. She has no history of bariatric surgery. She denies a personal history of pancreatitis, thyroid carcinoma, or multiple endocrine neoplasia (MEN2). She has no known drug allergies.
