## HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS

Mr. Robert M. Okafor is a 52-year-old male with a longstanding history of obesity (ICD-10: E66.09) and hypertension (ICD-10: I10) who presents to the endocrinology clinic for evaluation and initiation of weight management pharmacotherapy. His current BMI is 35 kg/m², meeting the threshold for Class II obesity. His obesity is not an isolated finding — it is a direct and contributing driver of his hypertension, which has proven difficult to control despite antihypertensive pharmacotherapy. His primary care physician initiated a referral to endocrinology given the metabolic complexity of his case and the need for a structured, specialist-guided weight management strategy.

Mr. Okafor has engaged in a medically supervised weight management program spanning approximately 18 months. Beginning in January 2024, he was formally enrolled in a structured behavioral and dietary intervention program supervised by a registered dietitian (RD) at [Clinic Name on file], with documented visits occurring on January 8, 2024; March 15, 2024; June 3, 2024; September 10, 2024; and December 2, 2024. Despite full adherence to a calorie-restricted diet (1,600–1,800 kcal/day, low-sodium, reduced saturated fat) and a structured aerobic exercise regimen (150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity activity as documented by his exercise physiologist), Mr. Okafor achieved a maximum weight loss of approximately 4 lbs over this 18-month period, which was ultimately regained. His dietitian's most recent note (December 2, 2024) explicitly documents "inadequate response to lifestyle intervention despite sustained effort and compliance" and recommends escalation to pharmacotherapy.

Prior pharmacotherapy for obesity was attempted beginning in February 2025. Mr. Okafor was initiated on orlistat 120 mg orally three times daily with meals. He tolerated the medication for approximately six weeks (February 10, 2025 through March 24, 2025), during which time he experienced significant gastrointestinal adverse effects including oily spotting, fecal urgency, and frequent oily stools, which substantially impaired his quality of life and occupational functioning. No meaningful weight loss was achieved during this period. Given both intolerability and lack of efficacy, orlistat was discontinued on March 24, 2025, as documented in the primary care visit note of that date. Phentermine/topiramate ER was subsequently considered but was deemed inappropriate given the patient's uncontrolled hypertension, as phentermine carries a contraindication in the setting of uncontrolled or labile blood pressure and cardiovascular risk. Bupropion/naltrexone (Contrave) was also considered; however, given Mr. Okafor's blood pressure profile averaging 148/92 mmHg at the time of evaluation, this combination was similarly determined to carry unacceptable cardiovascular risk, as the bupropion component is associated with blood pressure elevation. These clinical decisions are consistent with current obesity pharmacotherapy guidelines. Tirzepatide (Zepbound) is therefore the most appropriate remaining option in the GLP-1/GIP receptor agonist class, with demonstrated robust efficacy in obesity reduction and a favorable cardiovascular and blood pressure profile that is particularly relevant in this patient's clinical context.
