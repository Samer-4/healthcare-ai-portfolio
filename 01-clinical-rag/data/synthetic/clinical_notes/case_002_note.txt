## HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS

Mr. Robert M. Okafor is a 52-year-old male with a longstanding history of obesity and hypertension who presents to the Endocrinology clinic for specialist evaluation and pharmacologic management of his weight. He was referred by his primary care physician following failure to achieve clinically meaningful or sustained weight loss through repeated structured attempts at lifestyle modification over a period of approximately three years. His current BMI is 35 kg/m², meeting the threshold for Class II obesity per standard clinical classification. He reports a gradual weight increase over the past decade, which has accelerated over the past three to four years despite his awareness and engagement with dietary counseling and exercise programming.

Mr. Okafor has been participating in a structured behavioral weight-loss program since approximately January 2022. He engaged with a registered dietitian on a biweekly basis through June 2022, during which time he adopted a caloric restriction regimen targeting a daily deficit of 500–750 kcal, along with a supervised aerobic exercise protocol of 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity activity. Despite adherence to this regimen for a period of approximately six months, he achieved a peak weight reduction of only 6 lbs (approximately 2.7 kg), which was subsequently regained in full by October 2022. He resumed a modified lifestyle intervention in early 2023, including referral to a medically supervised low-calorie diet program. Once again, weight loss was minimal and unsustained, with no net change documented at his 12-month follow-up in February 2024. His primary care provider documented these interventions in the medical record, reflecting multiple serious, good-faith attempts at non-pharmacologic therapy prior to this referral.

Of significant clinical concern is the direct contribution of his obesity to his hypertension. Mr. Okafor was diagnosed with hypertension in 2018 and has required escalating antihypertensive pharmacotherapy over the intervening years. His blood pressure control remains suboptimal despite being on a two-drug regimen (see Current Medications), and his managing internist has attributed the difficulty in achieving target blood pressure goals in large part to his persistent excess adiposity. There is robust literature confirming the bidirectional relationship between obesity and hypertension, and sustained weight loss of 5–10% of body weight has been shown to result in meaningful reductions in systolic blood pressure, potentially allowing for de-escalation of antihypertensive therapy. Given Mr. Okafor's age, sex, and cardiovascular risk profile, pharmacologic weight management is medically necessary to reduce his risk of major adverse cardiovascular events, including stroke, myocardial infarction, and heart failure.
