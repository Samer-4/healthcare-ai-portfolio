## SUBJECTIVE

Ms. Jennifer L. Caldwell is a 45-year-old female with a well-established history of Class II obesity (BMI 38 kg/m²), type 2 diabetes mellitus (E11.9), and hypertension who presents today for follow-up and to support appeal of Aetna's denial of semaglutide (Wegovy) on the basis of step therapy not completed. I am writing this note to formally document that step therapy requirements have, in fact, been satisfied and that multiple prior-line agents have been trialed, optimized, and found to be either inadequate in efficacy or not tolerated.

**History of Present Illness:**

Ms. Caldwell was first diagnosed with type 2 diabetes mellitus in March 2018 and with obesity in the same clinical encounter. Since that time, she has been under my direct care and has engaged in a structured, progressive treatment plan targeting both glycemic control and meaningful weight reduction. She reports persistent difficulty achieving and maintaining a clinically meaningful weight loss of ≥5% body weight despite sustained and documented efforts with pharmacologic therapy, structured dietary intervention, and supervised physical activity programming.

She currently weighs 224 lbs (BMI 38 kg/m²) and reports that her weight has been essentially stable or trending upward over the past 18 months despite active treatment. She denies binge eating disorder. She reports low-grade fatigue, exertional dyspnea with moderate activity, and lower extremity edema bilaterally, all of which she and I attribute in part to her obesity and suboptimally controlled cardiometabolic risk burden.

**Step Therapy — Prior Medications Tried and Failed (Chronological):**

1. **Metformin (Glucophage) 500 mg twice daily → titrated to 1000 mg twice daily**
   - Initiated: April 2018
   - Duration: Ongoing (currently still prescribed for glycemic control)
   - Outcome: Adequate glycemic contribution (HbA1c modestly improved from 8.1% to 7.4%) but no clinically meaningful weight loss. Patient lost approximately 3 lbs over 12 months, which did not meet the threshold of ≥5% body weight reduction. Continued as background diabetes therapy.

2. **Lifestyle Intervention — Structured Program (Weight Watchers / WW)**
   - Initiated: June 2018
   - Duration: 12 months (through June 2019)
   - Outcome: Patient attended weekly sessions consistently for the first 6 months, transitioning to biweekly sessions thereafter. Net weight change at program end: −4 lbs (approximately 1.8% body weight). Deemed inadequate response. Patient remained committed but reported significant difficulty with sustained adherence due to work schedule and hunger dysregulation.

3. **Phentermine/Topiramate ER (Qsymia) 3.75 mg/23 mg → titrated to 7.5 mg/46 mg**
   - Initiated: August 2019
   - Duration: 6 months (discontinued February 2020)
   - Reason for Discontinuation: Patient developed symptomatic paresthesias in bilateral hands and feet and significant cognitive slowing ("brain fog") that impaired her work performance as a paralegal. She also reported dry mouth and insomnia. These adverse effects were intolerable and did not resolve with dose reduction. Medication discontinued after shared decision-making discussion. Net weight change: −6 lbs over 6 months (approximately 2.7% body weight), which was considered inadequate given the adverse effect burden.

4. **Naltrexone/Bupropion ER (Contrave) 8 mg/90 mg → titrated to 16 mg/180 mg twice daily (target dose)**
   - Initiated: May 2020
   - Duration: 9 months (discontinued February 2021)
   - Reason for Discontinuation: Patient developed nausea and persistent headaches during titration that partially resolved but did not fully abate. More significantly, she experienced a sustained elevation in blood pressure (readings consistently 148–156/92–98 mmHg) on Contrave, which was counterproductive given her concurrent hypertension diagnosis. After 9 months at therapeutic dose, weight loss totaled approximately 5 lbs (2.2% body weight), which was clinically insufficient. Medication discontinued due to combination of inadequate efficacy and worsening hypertensive effect.

5. **Liraglutide (Saxenda) 0.6 mg SQ daily → titrated to 3.0 mg SQ daily (maximum dose)**
   - Initiated: June 2021
   - Duration: 12 months (discontinued June 2022)

At 12-month follow-up, total weight loss was approximately 9 lbs (4.0% body weight). While liraglutide provided partial response and modest improvement in glycemic parameters (HbA1c 7.4% → 7.1%), weight loss remained below the clinically meaningful threshold of ≥5% body weight and was inadequate for a patient with a BMI of 38 kg/m² and active cardiometabolic comorbidities. Given the inadequate response at maximum dose after 12 months of therapy, a transition to a more efficacious agent was clinically indicated.
