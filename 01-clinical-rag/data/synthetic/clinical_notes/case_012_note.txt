## SUBJECTIVE

Ms. Linda S. Martinez is a 52-year-old female presenting today for follow-up regarding ongoing management of Class II obesity (BMI 37 kg/m²), hypertension, and obstructive sleep apnea, as well as to address Aetna's denial of tirzepatide (Zepbound) on the basis of step therapy not completed. This note serves as comprehensive documentation to support the appeal of that denial.

**History of Present Illness:**
Ms. Martinez has a well-documented, multi-year history of obesity and its associated comorbidities. She reports persistent difficulty with weight management despite sustained, good-faith efforts across multiple therapeutic modalities over the past several years. She notes progressive fatigue, exertional dyspnea on moderate exertion, disrupted sleep (currently using CPAP for OSA), and persistent elevated blood pressure despite pharmacologic management. She endorses joint pain in bilateral knees that she and I attribute, at least in part, to her excess weight. She expresses significant frustration with her inability to achieve durable weight loss and is highly motivated to pursue pharmacologic therapy as a complement to lifestyle interventions.

**Step Therapy Documentation — Prior Weight Loss Interventions (in chronological order):**

**1. Structured Dietary Intervention with Registered Dietitian (RD)**
- **Dates:** March 2022 – September 2022 (6 months)
- **Program:** Referred to and enrolled in a medically supervised, low-calorie dietary program with Sarah Okafor, RD, at Riverside Nutrition Center. Caloric target of 1,200–1,400 kcal/day; macronutrient-balanced meal plan.
- **Outcome:** Patient lost approximately 6 lbs. over the course of the program. Weight loss was not sustained; patient regained weight within 3 months of completing the program. Net weight change at 12-month mark: +2 lbs. from baseline. Program completion documented in RD records, copies available on request.

**2. Structured Exercise Program**
- **Dates:** April 2022 – December 2022 (8 months, overlapping with dietary intervention)
- **Program:** Enrolled in a supervised exercise program at ClearPath Wellness Center, 3 sessions per week, supervised by a certified exercise physiologist. Program included aerobic conditioning and resistance training tailored to her cardiovascular and orthopedic limitations.
- **Outcome:** Modest improvement in exercise tolerance. Minimal weight reduction achieved (approximately 4 lbs. at program nadir). Weight loss not maintained beyond program duration due to bilateral knee pain limiting high-impact activity. Patient transitioned to low-impact aquatic exercise independently but with inconsistent adherence due to scheduling constraints.

**3. Orlistat (Xenical) 120 mg TID with meals**
- **Dates:** February 2023 – August 2023 (6 months)
- **Prescribing Provider:** Dr. Brian Fenwick, MD
- **Rationale for initiation:** Initiated as first-line pharmacologic step therapy per Aetna formulary requirements and standard of care guidelines following failure of supervised dietary and exercise interventions.
- **Outcome:** Patient experienced significant gastrointestinal adverse effects including oily stools, fecal urgency, and fecal incontinence episodes, which were severely disruptive to her daily functioning and occupational responsibilities. Despite dietary fat restriction, side effects remained intolerable. Weight loss over the 6-month period was 5 lbs., which was clinically inadequate. Medication was discontinued in August 2023 due to intolerability. Discontinuation documented in prior encounter notes (08/14/2023).

**4. Phentermine/Topiramate ER (Qsymia) 3.75 mg/23 mg titrated to 7.5 mg/46 mg daily**
- **Dates:** November 2023 – April 2024 (6 months)
- **Prescribing Provider:** Dr. Brian Fenwick, MD
- **Rationale for initiation:** Initiated as second-line pharmacologic step therapy following orlistat failure/intolerance.
- **Outcome:** Patient tolerated initial titration but developed significant adverse effects at the maintenance dose including cognitive slowing ("brain fog"), word-finding difficulty, paresthesias in bilateral hands and feet, and mood lability. These side effects were functionally impairing, affecting her performance in her administrative management position. In addition, weight loss over the 6-month trial was approximately 8 lbs. (approximately 4.5% of body weight), which falls below the clinically meaningful threshold of ≥5% body weight reduction with adequate pharmacotherapy. Medication discontinued April 2024 due to intolerable neurocognitive side effects and inadequate response. Documented in encounter note (04/09/2024).

- **Dates:** July 2024 – December 2024 (6 months)
- **Prescribing Provider:** Dr. Brian Fenwick, MD
- **Rationale for initiation:** Initiated as third-line pharmacologic step therapy following failure and intolerance of orlistat and phentermine/topiramate ER.
- **Outcome:** Patient experienced persistent nausea, headache, and elevated blood pressure on Contrave (average BP readings on medication: 148/94 mmHg, compared to baseline on antihypertensive therapy of approximately 138/88 mmHg). Given her underlying hypertension (ICD-10: I10), the additional blood pressure burden was clinically unacceptable and the medication was discontinued in December 2024. Weight loss over 6-month trial: approximately 6 lbs.
