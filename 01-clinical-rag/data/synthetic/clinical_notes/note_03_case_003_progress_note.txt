## SUBJECTIVE

**History of Present Illness:**
Ms. Maria T. Reyes is a 38-year-old Hispanic female presenting today for a dedicated visit to address her longitudinal struggle with obesity (BMI 33 kg/m²) and prediabetes (fasting glucose and HbA1c documented below), and to discuss initiation of semaglutide (Ozempic) following a prior authorization denial citing insufficient documentation. The purpose of this note is to provide a comprehensive and exhaustive account of her clinical history, comorbidity burden, and prior treatment efforts.

**Weight and Metabolic History:**
Ms. Reyes reports a progressive weight gain beginning in her mid-20s, initially attributed to two pregnancies (G2P2, deliveries in 2012 and 2015). She describes persistent difficulty with weight management for approximately 10 years, with her highest recorded weight of 198 lbs (BMI ~34) documented in June 2023. Her current weight is 192 lbs (BMI 33 kg/m²). She denies any secondary causes of weight gain but endorses fatigue, increased hunger, carbohydrate cravings, and difficulty with satiety, which she reports have worsened over the past 18 months.

**Prediabetes History:**
Ms. Reyes was formally diagnosed with prediabetes (ICD-10: R73.09) in March 2023 following two confirmatory fasting glucose values of 108 mg/dL and 111 mg/dL on separate occasions, and an HbA1c of 6.1% (March 2023). Repeat HbA1c in October 2024 was 6.3%, indicating progression within the prediabetes range and raising concern for impending conversion to type 2 diabetes mellitus. She has a strong family history of type 2 diabetes mellitus, with her mother diagnosed at age 44 and her maternal grandmother requiring insulin therapy. She does not currently carry a diagnosis of T2DM.

**Obesity History:**
Ms. Reyes meets criteria for Class I obesity (ICD-10: E66.01) with a BMI of 33 kg/m². Her obesity is persistent and documented across multiple visits over the past 3+ years.

**Prior Non-Pharmacologic Treatment Attempts (Documented and Exhaustive):**

1. **Structured Low-Calorie Diet Program (March 2023 – September 2023):**
Referred to a registered dietitian (RD) through our clinic's nutrition services department. Patient attended 8 individual sessions over 6 months. A 1,500 kcal/day Mediterranean-style diet was prescribed with carbohydrate restriction targeting <150g/day to address prediabetes. Patient was compliant per self-report and RD documentation. Total weight lost: 6 lbs over 6 months (3% of body weight). Weight was regained within 3 months of program completion. HbA1c did not significantly improve (6.1% → 6.1%).

2. **Structured Exercise Program (April 2023 – ongoing, intermittent):**
Patient was counseled to engage in at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise per week, consistent with American Diabetes Association (ADA) guidelines for prediabetes management. She enrolled in a community fitness program in April 2023 and maintained consistent attendance for approximately 5 months. She reports difficulty sustaining exercise frequency due to bilateral knee pain (consistent with BMI-related mechanical stress) and work schedule constraints (full-time employment, single-parent household). Average sustained weight loss attributable to exercise: minimal (<2 lbs). She continues to attempt exercise 2–3 times per week but acknowledges inconsistency.

3. **Diabetes Prevention Program (DPP) Participation (January 2024 – July 2024):**
Ms. Reyes was formally enrolled in a CDC-recognized Diabetes Prevention Program (virtual cohort) beginning January 2024. She completed 22 of 26 core sessions over 6 months. The program included structured behavioral coaching, goal setting, dietary modification, and supervised physical activity guidance. Despite high engagement, she achieved a total weight loss of 5 lbs (2.6% of body weight) by program end. The DPP target of ≥5–7% body weight reduction was not achieved. HbA1c at program end (July 2024): 6.2%. Her DPP case coordinator noted in the discharge summary that "patient is highly motivated but demonstrates physiologic resistance to weight loss consistent with hormonal and metabolic contributors."

4. **Metformin Trial (August 2024 – November 2024):**
Given rising HbA1c and failure to achieve target weight loss through lifestyle interventions, metformin 500 mg twice daily was initiated in August 2024 for prediabetes management and modest weight-loss support. Dose was uptitrated to 1,000 mg twice daily by September 2024. Patient tolerated the medication initially but reported persistent GI adverse effects including nausea, bloating, and diarrhea at the higher dose, which impacted her quality of life and work performance. Metformin was reduced back to 500 mg twice daily in October 2024 in an attempt to improve tolerability; however, weight loss was negligible (total loss: 1.5 lbs over 3 months). HbA1c in October 2024: 6.3% — representing continued progression despite therapy. Metformin was discontinued in November 2024 due to inadequate efficacy and sustained GI intolerance even at the lower dose. Documentation of this trial is available in the medication administration record and clinical notes.
